**Reading Notes on the Elon Musk Biography: The Five-Step Method**

1. **Question every requirement.** Every requirement should be attached to a person’s name. Never accept a requirement from "legal" or "safety"—you must know the individual responsible. Question it, no matter how smart they are. Requirements from smart people are the most dangerous because they are less likely to be questioned. Even if it comes from Musk himself, question and improve it.
2. **Delete as much as you can.** You can always add it back later. If you don't end up adding back at least 10% of what you deleted, you haven't deleted enough.
3. **Simplify and optimize.** This MUST come after step 2. People often make the mistake of optimizing a process that shouldn't even exist.
4. **Accelerate cycle time.** Every process can be faster, but only after following the first three steps. At Tesla, I once spent too much energy speeding up a process that should have been removed entirely.
5. **Automate.** A big mistake at the Nevada and Fremont factories was trying to automate every step from the start. We should have questioned requirements, deleted unnecessary parts, and fixed issues before automating.

**The Method Sometimes Leads to a Few Corollaries**

1. All technical managers must have hands-on experience. Managers of software teams, for example, must spend at least 20% of their time coding, and managers of the solar roof business must spend time on roofs doing installations themselves. Otherwise they are all talk, like a cavalry captain who cannot ride or a general who cannot use a sword.
2. "You're fine, I'm fine, everyone's fine" is dangerous, because it makes people stop questioning their colleagues' work. People naturally do not want to throw a good colleague overboard, and that dangerous tendency must be avoided.
3. Making mistakes is fine, but being wrong and refusing to admit it is not.
4. Never ask your team to do something you are not willing to do yourself.
5. Whenever there is a problem to solve, do not only talk to the managers who report to you. To dig in properly, communicate across levels and talk directly to the people who report to your reports.
6. Hire people with the right attitude. Skills can be taught, but changing someone's attitude to work is far too hard, it takes "a brain transplant". A maniacal sense of urgency is the operating principle of our company.
7. The only rules that must be followed are the ones that can be derived from the laws of physics. Everything else is a recommendation.
