## Random Thoughts

### How to Get Good Coffee Every Day for Under 1,000 RMB

<img src="https://gw.alipayobjects.com/zos/k/fm/IMG_7802.jpg" width="800" />

How do you get good coffee every day for under 1,000 RMB? I want to use this post to share my experience buying and using a coffee machine and choosing its accessories, the purchase that has added the most to my daily life lately.

☕️ Almost every morning lately, the first thing I do after getting up is grind coffee beans, pull a shot on the semi-automatic machine, froth half a cup of milk and pour it into the coffee for a full cup. With the morning sun coming in through the window, the slightly bitter, rich taste of the crema mixed with the sweetness of the milk is deeply satisfying. Over a month I used very few beans and only a few cartons of milk, far cheaper than buying Starbucks, and more importantly, making it by hand and the taste beat buying a coffee by a mile. The machine paid for itself within a month.

🤖 There are several kinds of coffee machines. Capsule machines are simple and fast, and fully automatic ones are feature-rich and easy, you just press a button. I went with a semi-automatic machine, the De'Longhi ECP33.21.W from Sam's Club, only 629 RMB. The white metal body feels solid, and it has just two functions, extraction and frothing, and it is not hard to use. I chose semi-automatic mostly because I like doing it by hand, and the simple feature set also makes it easy to clean. I think it is a great machine for beginners, well built and durable.

🫘 I also bought the beans at Sam's Club. After checking recommendations on Xiaohongshu, I got the MM Classic Italian Espresso Blend, 1kg for 99 RMB, the one in the orange-yellow bag. The moment I opened it and poured it into the storage jar felt so, so, so good, the beans plump and oily with crema aroma, and that smell of coffee still stays with me. It also tastes better than I expected, not too sour, not too bitter, just rich and smooth, and after almost a month I have barely used any.

🥛 With milk I knew from the start that I had to buy one that suits coffee, and whole milk is more fragrant. The first one I tried was Mengniu fresh milk from Sam's Club, 2L for only 20 RMB, great value, and I thought it tasted pretty good, but it is simply too much, a cup a day is hard to finish before it expires. Later I tried Meiji's rich milk from Hema, which is smooth and full on the palate and also good. Then I tried Yoplait 4.0, which many coffee friends recommended. Hema was out of stock, so I bought 5.0, also very good, its sweetness with the coffee feels just right. I will probably keep using these few.

🎻 As for accessories, the one thing not to forget is a grinder. I chose an electric one. At first I thought a hand grinder would feel more like a ritual, but then I decided it was not worth it, espresso needs a very fine grind and grinding by hand is exhausting. Once the coffee is ground, it needs to be leveled and tamped firmly. The machine came with only a spoon and a plastic tool that doubles as a tamper, which felt a bit cheap, so I got a metal spoon. To make the process feel more like a ritual, I also bought a distributor and a tamper, which make the coffee bed flatter and more compact. I see them as nice-to-haves, the spoon that came with the machine is fine too, but these are better. Then I bought a metal milk pitcher, which is far better than a glass, easy to pour and to feel the temperature. Later I bought a small brush, a coffee cloth and a knock box. These three little things are cheap but super handy, much, much better than cleaning up with paper as I did before.

👷‍♀️ As for making the coffee, I have not learned to pour pretty latte art yet, it just tastes good. When extracting, be careful not to run it too long. The only part that takes some technique is frothing silky milk, which you can learn and practice from Xiaohongshu. My trick is to put the steam wand at the center first, then tilt it toward three o'clock so the milk spins fast, turn it off once the pitcher is too hot to hold, then pour it into the coffee at an angle.

😄 All in all, before buying I was sure it would end up gathering dust. After buying, I found I have actually gotten used to making a cup of coffee to greet me every morning, and it even gives me a reason to get out of bed, much like in college, when what got me up every day was looking forward to a freshly made roujiamo and fresh soy milk at the canteen. When friends come over, I can't wait to make them a cup to try my skills, and that feels pretty good.
